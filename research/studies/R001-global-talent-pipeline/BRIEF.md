# R001 — Global participation and talent pipeline

**Prepared:** 2026-10-31  
**Research status:** RESEARCHED (initial Gemini report and two audits archived; NOT independently VERIFIED or approved)  
**Scope:** cross-version research; can inform current global squash world and later optional Run scenarios, but commits nothing to pre-alpha  
**Researcher:** Gemini 4 Argon / Deep Research candidate; independent review required

## Goal

Evaluate scientifically plausible mechanisms for:

`population → squash participation/access → prospect pool → identification → development/competition/support → professional conversion → elite performance`.

Squash Engine simulates an **alternative world with genuinely global squash**. It should produce geographically uneven but credible competition, rare greats and natural shifts among countries. Do **not** directly copy the present real-world country dominance in squash.

## Research questions

1. How do participating population, availability and talent identification affect the rate of discovered elite prospects?
2. How does sporting ecosystem quality alter the **realization** of globally distributed innate potential?
3. What credible nonlinear population/participation effects should be evaluated, and where does empirical evidence end?
4. How do individual-player outliers and rare small-country champions emerge without national hard caps or forced equalization?
5. How can persistent country advantages and historical change coexist with world-level stability over 50–100 seasons?
6. Can a future **optional** Run parameter for global sport size and popularity evolution be supported without hard-coding it into the pre-alpha model?

## Deliverables and guardrails

- Evidence register with sources, year, DOI/URL, evidential strength and cross-sport transfer limitations.
- Three competing modelling approaches, explicit assumptions, trade-offs and uncertainties.
- A measurable calibration and long-run test plan, with attention to rare events and sample size.
- A small, prioritized set of candidate improvements and explicit owner questions.
- Before starting full Deep Research, submit the **research plan** for review.
- Do not code, change canonical rules or treat speculative suggestions as approved features.
- Store reviewed findings alongside this brief later; do not imply they exist now.
